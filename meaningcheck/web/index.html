What did not work

  • A plain "make it clear" rewrite by the same model also reached 100% on these tests; Meaning Check's added value is the check itself and shorter fixes, not better wording on every message.
  • The first Muse Spark run was void because of a scoring bug (a non-answer counted as understanding); it was fixed, rerun, and both runs are kept.
  • The Muse Glimmer panel's flags did not fully transfer to Muse Spark, which is why every check now uses both models.
  • Readers are AI models given backgrounds, not people; a human study was prepared but not run.

How a check works

  1. Readings. The writer lists what your message could be taken to mean, and the facts that tell those readings apart.
  2. Two panels read it. 16 Muse Glimmer readers (four countries, two jobs, quick and careful reasoning) give a probability for every reading, from the answer letters in one forward pass, averaged over two option orders. 8 Muse Spark readers give a second opinion. A message is misread if either model misreads it.
  3. Say enough, exactly. An integer programme finds the fewest facts that rule out every reading some reader preferred, and proves nothing shorter works.
  4. Write and re-read. The writer turns the facts into candidate wordings; both models re-read them, and the shortest wording the most readers understand is kept.
  5. Readers never used. Eight further readers, never used to build the fix, test it.

The theory behind it

A message is a set of statements m about the intended reading w. Stating attribute a rules out the readings that differ from w on it, Ea(w). A reader acts on the reading it finds most plausible among those not ruled out; its rivals Domπ(w) are the readings it finds at least as plausible as w.

Audience theorem. Every reader in a panel R understands m exactly when ⋃a∈m Ea(w) ⊇ ⋃π∈R Domπ(w). The shortest such m is a minimum set cover: NP-hard in general, small in practice, solved exactly here.

Sampling guarantee. With 95% confidence, a message all T sampled readers understand is misread by a new reader from the same population with probability at most (A ln 2 + ln 20)/T, for A candidate statements.
